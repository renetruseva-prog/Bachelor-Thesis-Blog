## Coming up with content

Anyone who has every had to create any type of website, application, software or an overall digital experience knows how hard it is to actually define the actual content, and not miss the small features that make an application UX-friendly, intuitive and easy to use for people of all expertise levels. In order to make it easier for ourselves, we applied three usefull UX/UI strategies.

## Minimal content

The minimal content is the foundational content that almost every site or software in our industry includes. We researched competitor softwares and interfaces to make sure we don't miss any essential features, menus, panels or overall functionality, and we came up with a list for our minimal content for each step of the project mapping process.

## Feature list

After we defined our minimal content list, we moved onto creating a feature list for each step of the process. In short, a feature list is a content list based on the target audience's specific needs, as well as useful features that are not part of the minimal content list. This is it:

Once we had defined all possible features we could brainstorm and think of, we used the MoSCoW method to prioritize and categorize the features.The acronym stands for M — Must have (non-negotiable / critical), S — Should have ( important and high-value features), C — Could have ( desirable, nice-to-have features) and W — Won't have (features that are currently out of the scope of the project).
