## Feedback on concept from the client

On Tuesday (10/11) we received feedback on our Web-Oo concept from our client. Barco really liked our ideas and the tools we included in our concept pitch deck, but they found the spider concept itself too confusing and inappropriate for some users. We were advised to go with a more neutral concept (design-wise. They told us that with more suitable naming not related to spiders like "no thread broken" -> troubleshooting or health, it will be clearer for the user what exactly should be found under a certain functionality. But overall, aside from the design choice of adding an assistant "character", they liked and validated the concepts we described under the tools, and they agreed that the different tools we chose definitely make sense. 

Upon receiving this feedback, we discussed the concept once again and created new sketches with a more clean, user-appropriate symbols. We removed the entire idea of using a character, and stuck to a more neutral "ToolBox" menu meant to help the users with their questions and errors as they go on a learning journey.

We also made our sketches fit the new cleaned-up concept, and used AI to create a quick lo-fi wireframe of how the tool box could look:

## Feedback from consult (Design)

We had a design consult where we showed our design inspiration board and our styleboard. We received feedback that the high contrast of colorus is a good choice for our interface, since the software will most likely be viewed outside and the interface should be visable in the dark. We also received feedback that red probably shouldn't be  a primary colour since it's traditionally associated with error handling - so we decided to use it as our 10% hightlight color to show and diagnose errors. We were advised to test the 2 different colors to see to which ones the users respond the best, and we plan to do that in the future.

## Feedback from consult (UI/UX)

At our UX consult we received feedback that:
- The personas have to be based on the research - (ours were based mostly on social listening, so we ended up doing more desk research to validate the personas' backgrounds)
- We need to talk to our client about possibly conducting interviews with their employees
- We should broaden up our research on what people find irritating, how long it takes to learn a new software, what's the learning curve for IT softwares, if we can skip certain steps in the learning process for this software etc
- We should already start doing small experiments with specific tools and functions, and do more desk research

Based on this feedback we started to gather more desk research on the topic of learning. First, we took a deeper look at irritations and pain points people currently have while using projection mapping softwares. These were our insights:

![user pain points and frustrations](./images/painpoints-2.png)

Then we looked at how long it takes in general to learn project mapping:

![how long it takes to learn projection mapping?](./images/learning.png)

Then we decided to focus on 6 main UX research questions/topics:
1. How people learn - Cognitive psychology, learning theories and skill acquisition
2. The learning curve - How people progress from complete beginners to experienced users?
3. The starting barrier - What makes people hesitant, overwhelmed or unwilling to learn?
4. User frustrations - What makes learning software confusing, exhausting or discouraging?
5. Beginner-friendly software design - How companies structure complex software for different experience levels?
6. Exploration of 3D software
